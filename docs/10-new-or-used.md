# New or Used: When Each One Wins

> The calendar, the price, and the risk are not three separate questions - they are one question: *which failure would you rather carry?* This chapter is the framework for answering it, and a few honest cases where "new with warranty" beats every used bargain you could find.

## The cost-of-ownership frame

A device costs what you pay **plus what you will spend to keep it alive**. Once you price both halves, the used-vs-new decision gets calmer, because the numbers are comparisons instead of feelings.

A worked example, laptop flavor:

| | Path A: used | Path B: new |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price paid | $280 (2-year-old mid-tier) | $750 |
| Battery soon | $90 replacement risk at ~80% | Included for years |
| Warranty | None | 1 year (plus whatever card you bought it on) |
| Insurance/protective case | $25 either way | $25 either way |
| Total year one | ~$370-395 | ~$775+ |
| Payoff if it breaks at month 7 | A repair bill or a new hunt | A warranty claim and a replacement |

The used path wins on year-one money and needs you to accept risk; the new path wins on certainty and resale chain-of-custody. Both are *right answers for different people*. Nothing in this chapter tells you which - it tells you which question you are answering.

## When new-with-warranty is genuinely the smarter buy

- **The device is load-bearing.** This phone is your two-factor, your safety backup, your work tool. A broken everyday phone costs you trust, time, and attention from every direction; a broken weekend tablet costs you a shrug. Path choice follows weight.
- **Update runway matters.** Security updates on phones run 5-7 years on the big platforms now, and laptops bought new get even longer support. Buying a phone in year 5 of its cycle is buying a device with 2 years of life left at 40% of the price - fine as a second device; thin reasoning for a primary.
- **The repair ecosystem is unfriendly.** A glued-battery flagship with 74% battery is almost priced like a newer one once the battery work is added - compare the official replacement price before feeling clever about the discount. Sometimes the "deal" is just the expensive part, arranged in advance.
- **You need guaranteed returns.** Gifts, first-day-of-class, project deadlines, the "I cannot tinker this weekend" window: the return policy *is* the product.
- **Burn-in-watching and niche specs.** Used OLED monitors or gaming panels with uncertain hours/usage history are the one category where new-with-warranty regularly beats the used market: the used premium is small, and failure modes are expensive.

## When used quietly wins

- **Second devices.** A couch tablet, a kids' music machine, a kitchen-recipe screen, a garage PDF. New upgrades for these are luxury spending; used is the honest price of a "backup instrument".
- **Monitors.** Office panels barely fail in five years, and the market floods with them. Buy comped, test per [the monitor test](06-test-drives.md#monitor-test-10-12-minutes), pocket the difference.
- **The best-price playbook.** 1-2 year old devices (Guide 5's band) are the sweet spot: somebody else ate the worst depreciation, the battery is usually 85% or better (per [Battery Health](02-battery-health.md)), and the device is on current software for years (per [After You Buy](11-after-you-buy.md)).
- **Devices you can test in person.** The whole risk model improves when the seller unlocks, demos, and wipes in front of you. Private-used with a good test beats both no-warranty refurbs and impulse market orders.

## The middle paths (often the best answer)

Between "random stranger at the parking lot" and "brand new, boxed" sits a useful middle ground with different risk profiles:

| Path | What it is | Where it wins |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Manufacturer certified refurbished** | Rebuilt, tested, battery at original spec, with a real warranty | Phones and laptops you would trust with real work |
| **Reputable refurbisher (online)** | Used-but-inspected with a return window; quality varies by seller | When you cannot test in person |
| **Open-box retail** | Customer returns, often unopened or barely used | Ask *why* it was opened ("display model" is great; unknown is a test you must run) |

Apple certified refurbished in particular includes a new battery and outer shell plus a warranty - and on the right models runs measurably above "used" but measurably below "new": a nice middle path where someone else's quality control covers part of the risk. Read the fine print of whoever refurbishes, every time.

## Trade-in vs sell-private

If your plan is "sell my old one and buy new": selling privately usually nets 20-50% more than a trade-in for recent, popular models - at the cost of the steps in this guide (wipe, listings, meetups, comps). Trade-in wins when the device is old, worn, damaged, or your time is more valuable than the difference. Both are disciplined choices; only the "no effort, no check" trade-in by default is not.

## A five-question gut-check

Answer these out loud. Four yeses lean used; three or fewer lean new-with-warranty.

1. Have I got the time for one meetup and an evening of setup?
2. Do I have (or could I get) a backup device for the weeks I'd be repair-bound?
3. Is this a primary device for work, safety, or banking? (Here the *opposite* of the usual logic applies - see above.)
4. Have I already seen two recent sold comps at 55%+ of new price?
5. Does the math still win after the *known* repair-items are added (battery, screen, case)?

Whatever you choose, the chapters behind you are the same toolkit: listings (Guide 1), locks (Guide 4), tests (Guide 6), wipes (Guide 9). New has less *hunting* but the same *owning*.
