# Read This First

> A calm manual for buying used laptops, phones, tablets, and monitors - written the way a friend would explain it. No hype, no fear, no affiliate links.

## Who this is for

- You are looking at secondhand listings and can smell the risk: the deal that looks perfect, the phone that is "totally fine", the laptop that needs "just a small screen fix".
- You want to buy used anyway, because it is sensible - half the price, on-the-spot inspection, no shipping gamble.
- You are not an expert and you do not want to become one. You want a short, reliable routine to follow, and permission to walk away.
- You may also be the one **selling**: the wiping rules here (Guide 9) protect your accounts, photos, and money on the way out.

Everything here is written for one anxiety-free principle: **information before money, money before attachment, and walking away at any point.** A used-tech transaction has no deadline. Nobody needs this phone this minute. The listings will still be there next week.

## The five golden rules

1. **You are the money.** Nothing leaves your pocket until you have held the device, unlocked it, and run the tests in these chapters.
2. **Locks come before price.** Activation Lock, Factory Reset Protection, and enterprise Chromebooks are bricks with nice screens. Check Guide 4 before admiring anything.
3. **The battery is the engine.** Its health moves the real price more than the color of the case (Guide 2).
4. **Wipe it twice.** The seller wipes before handoff; the buyer wipes again the same evening, just in case (Guide 9).
5. **Walking away is free.** There is always another listing. Always. If a deal feels rushed, that *is* information.

## Conventions and honest fine print

- **Word-for-word script boxes** appear in Guides 1, 6, 7, and 9. Say them plainly; they are written quietly on purpose.
- **Checklists** in the Worksheets folder use real checkboxes when the site is built, and are meant to be printed or copied to your phone notes.
- **Prices** in Guide 5 are US-market ballparks for orientation. Prices age fast and vary by region. The method (percent-of-new bands plus sold comps) is the durable part; treat any specific number with a sense of humor.
- Several checks reference Settings menus by their current names (as of early 2026). Menus get renamed; the flow almost never does. If a menu moved, it moved to somewhere nearby.
- Nothing here is financial, security, or legal advice. It is one person's calm routine, shared with you.

## What is deliberately not in this guide

Buying is the first half of owning used tech. This guide stops where the machine becomes yours:

- Reviving an old laptop after purchase - **backup, calm wipe, lightweight Linux, Ollama, the spare-room station** - is the job of the sibling manual, the **Old Laptop Revival Guide**. Where that guide *revives*, this guide *decides and acquires*. When you get a great deal on a tired ThinkPad at Guide 11, that guide is your next read.
- Running local AI models, choosing distros, or tuning hardware. Covered there, not here, so these chapters stay about *buying*.

## A five-minute taste

Here is the entire method in 90 seconds:

1. Read the listing like a detective (Guide 1). Dealbreakers: still signed in, "locked to owner, forgot password", shipped-only.
2. Message the seller with three plain questions (Guide 1): exact model and storage, why selling, battery percentage.
3. Comp the price (Guide 5): three sold listings of the same model, same storage. Write the range down.
4. Meet in a busy public place in daylight (Guide 8) with the printed worksheet (Worksheets folder).
5. Test in order: hardware with it off, unlock in your sight, About screen, locks (Guide 4), screen (Guide 3), battery (Guide 2), our test-drive order (Guide 6).
6. Make one calm offer anchored to real comps (Guide 7) - or say the walk-away line and leave with your cash.
7. Wipe it again that evening (Guide 9), set it up fresh, and update it.

That is the whole discipline. The chapters below just make each step slow enough to be safe and fast enough to still be human.
