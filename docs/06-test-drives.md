# Test Drives: What to Check at the Meetup

> A test drive is a conversation with a script. You know what you will check, in what order, how long it takes, and what each result means for the price. That script is this chapter, and it ends with you either paying with confidence or walking away with your whole budget.

## Before you leave the house

Pack like a friend, not a detective:

- [ ] The printed worksheet for this device kind (see the [Worksheets](worksheets/laptop-worksheet.md) folder) or the list in your phone notes.
- [ ] Exact-ish cash in small bills. Nothing sours a good price like "can you break a hundred?"
- [ ] Your own charger and cable. A seller's flaky cable that charges nothing is a classic way to misjudge a perfectly good battery.
- [ ] Your phone with a working hotspot, so you can test Wi-Fi-independent tasks and never connect to the seller's home network.
- [ ] A USB stick carrying the [offline color test](assets/color-test.html) and a couple of small files (a PDF or photo to test file transfer on laptops).
- [ ] A pen. Worksheet answers get written down by pen. Pen is honest.

Two habits worth more than any tool: screenshot or write down the listing's photos (so you notice if the device arrives different), and re-read the seller's exact words *right* before you meet.

## The order of operations

The order exists because it front-loads the tests that end deals early:

1. **Hardware, device off.** Before it powers on: corners, edges, screws (tool marks = opened), charging port lint, buttons that click properly, hinge travel, back cover gaps, serial stickers intact. Add two minutes of no-drama inspection - scratches, dents, who-owned-this wear - because devices tell their true history *before* they boot.
2. **The unlock happens in your sight.** The seller types their own password. "It's dead, let me charge it" is not unreasonable; a no-name cable that charges nothing for ten minutes is. Ten minutes is reasonable; an hour is a decision point.
3. **The About screen.** Model, storage, battery percentage, serial/IMEI - photographed by *you* (you will store them with your records of the purchase; see [After You Buy](11-after-you-buy.md)).
4. **Lock checks, in full**, from [Locks, Accounts, and Seed Phrases](04-locks-and-seed-phrases.md). Accounts and reset in your presence, complete before anything else matters.
5. **Screen battery and tests**, from the chapters dedicated to them ([Battery](02-battery-health.md), [Screen Checks](03-screen-checks.md)).
6. **Device-specific everything.** The checklists below cover the fields, ports, buttons, radios, and cameras. This is the bulk of the visit time.
7. **The wipe right there** (for phones and tablets): reset happens after all tests pass and before money moves, then you watch it arrive again at the "Hello" screen.

Then: pay, thank them, take the (wiped) device, leave. The reset step and payment order is: tests pass → agree the number → they reset while you stand there → money. Never canceled tests after money.

## Laptop test (12-15 minutes)

Bring it to a table, sit down, be unhurried.

- [ ] Boots from full off. An SSD-era machine reaches the desktop in well under a minute; a slow old hard drive is slow, not broken - price the speed, don't panic at the boot.
- [ ] Keyboard: every key pressed in a typing-line test (holding a blank document and typing q-p, q-p across the rows shows double-registers or dead keys fast)
- [ ] Trackpad: full four corners + click, **and both physical click corners if it has them**
- [ ] Screen: [Screen Checks](03-screen-checks.md) routine at full brightness, plus the dead-corner read on white backgrounds
- [ ] Battery: macOS = System Report/Cycle Count; Windows = `powercfg /batteryreport` while you talk (the command finishes as the conversation goes on); note FULL vs DESIGN capacities on your sheet
- [ ] Sleep does sleep: close the lid, count to ten, reopen - it should wake quickly, with no frozen screen
- [ ] Fan: run two or three big things at once (a video, a big web page, a document with a spreadsheet); fans may spin up but should not howl for 10 minutes straight
- [ ] Heat: palm on the keyboard and thumb on the bottom plate; warm is fine, too-hot-to-rest is a thumbs-down
- [ ] Ports: your USB stick reads in every Type-A port; charge something from each USB-C port (ports fail one at a time, so test them one at a time); headphone jack plays audio if present
- [ ] Sound: play audio through the speakers, both sides, no crackle at moderate volume; camera opens and focuses; microphone records and plays back clearly
- [ ] Wireless: joins your hotspot quickly and holds for several minutes without dropping (most Wi-Fi module faults show up right here)
- [ ] Bluetooth: pairs with your headphones in seconds and holds the connection
- [ ] System info screen: storage type and size, and the shown RAM, match the listing
- [ ] Only after everything passes: the wipe ritual from [chapter 4](04-locks-and-seed-phrases.md) happens in your presence, or - for many Windows machines - the clean install happens at your place with a USB stick (see [Wipe It](09-wipe-it.md))

Walk or discount triggers specific to laptops: lifting hinges, battery below 70% capacity on a 2-year-old machine, fans that howl at idle, Wi-Fi that drops, keyboard with dead keys, and any BIOS password the seller cannot remove on the spot.

## Phone test (8-12 minutes)

The most expensive things you will ever buy used, and the easiest to over-test. Eight to twelve minutes covers the whole phone.

- [ ] **Before touch:** corners and edges, screen-off scratch hunt, charging port working with your cable
- [ ] About screen: model, storage, carrier status, IMEI photographed; battery health % on camera
- [ ] Lock checks per [chapter 4](04-locks-and-seed-phrases.md) - full, on the spot, complete reset in your presence
- [ ] All physical buttons: volume pair, mute switch, side button (press with the screen off AND the case both on and off, if a case is included)
- [ ] Camera set: photo (front and back), video with audio, flash on, portrait mode if advertised; zoom levels for multi-lens phones
- [ ] Audio: earpiece speaker (a call on speakerphone to your own phone's voicemail or a friend), main speaker, microphone (record a voice memo, play it back), vibration and haptics
- [ ] Cellular: insert *your* SIM briefly and make a two-second call, then check 4G/5G data. If juggling SIMs is not practical today, at least watch the phone's own carrier bars while the seller's SIM is in, and confirm data works - radio problems almost always announce themselves immediately.
- [ ] Wi-Fi and Bluetooth: joins your hotspot; pairs with your headphones
- [ ] Charging: your cable, and watch the percentage climb 2-3% in ten minutes at a wall charger or your power bank (choppy movement or stuck numbers are battery clues)
- [ ] Touch sweep and screen tests per [Screen Checks](03-screen-checks.md), including True Tone on an iPhone
- [ ] The reset ritual per chapter 4, to the "Hello" screen, before payment

Discount triggers: cellular radio problems, an earpiece or speaker that crackles on calls, Touch ID or Face ID misbehaving (or missing entirely on replacement screens), battery under 85% on non-flagships, and - always - previous-owner cloud accounts that will not drop.

## Tablet test (8-10 minutes)

Tablets are phones that gave up the "phone" duties, and the tests follow:

- [ ] About screen, battery percentage, and model/storage/storage-type answers for your sheet
- [ ] Screen: [Screen Checks](03-screen-checks.md); burn-in shows up fast on mid-gray if it has AMOLED panels; the touch sweep is *especially* important on the edges you plan to flick with your thumb
- [ ] Buttons and speakers: tablets live or die on stereo speakers - test both sides at moderate volume
- [ ] Cameras: video calls are tablets' real job at home; test the front camera and its mic
- [ ] Wi-Fi: joins your hotspot; hold it for several minutes of scrolling
- [ ] Pen (if advertised): strokes straight lines, then diagonals, then the *edges*; pressure-sensitive lines get thinner and thicker as an Apple Pencil or active stylus tilts and presses
- [ ] Keyboard folio (if advertised): magnetic connection snaps in, every keypress registers on the screen, touchpad cursor wanders all corners
- [ ] Cellular model? Same cellular checks as phones, using your SIM or the seller's plan
- [ ] Reset to the "Hello" screen per [chapter 4](04-locks-and-seed-phrases.md), then pay

## Monitor test (10-12 minutes)

The most over-looked checks in used tech - and the easiest to do well, because you bring the whole test rig.

- [ ] **Your machine drives it.** Your laptop, your cable, your [color test](assets/color-test.html) on a USB stick. Never test a monitor by "it turns on and shows something" through the seller's mystery cable.
- [ ] Every port you plan to use: plug in each one (HDMI, DisplayPort, USB-C if advertised), watch the input switch in the OSD
- [ ] Full scale of brightness on and low; [Screen Checks](03-screen-checks.md) uniformity/dead-pixel/bleed suite
- [ ] **Refresh claims** (if rated 120Hz+): set the OS refresh rate to the max and scroll a long page; a 60Hz panel pretending to be 144Hz is instantly visible in a fast scroll
- [ ] Speakers (if it has them) play both sides; audio crackle at moderate volume is a discount, not a mystery
- [ ] OSD buttons: tap every physical button; sticky menus and dead buttons promise years of small irritation
- [ ] Stand: full height range, tilt, and any swivel used smoothly; wobble is normal on cheap stands, a stand that will not hold its position is not. VESA holes present if you plan a mount.
- [ ] Warm it up for the first ten minutes of conversation, then look again: ghosting, tint shift, or lines that appear only after warmup (a tired driver board or dying capacitors announce themselves in exactly this window)
- [ ] USB hub pass-through (if provided): your stick reads through it

Monitors are the one device where "new with warranty" sometimes beats used prices by a small margin; see [New or Used](10-new-or-used.md).

## The reset-then-pay ritual

For phones, tablets, and laptops, the cleanest choreography of payment:

1. All tests passed; nobody is under time pressure.
2. Agree the final number out loud. "For 215, final, and we reset right here?"
3. The seller wipes the device *in your presence*, per their platform's steps from [Wipe It](09-wipe-it.md).
4. The device arrives at "Hello"/a clean installer; **you tap through the first setup screen**, verify no old accounts appear, then *finish the setup at home*.
5. Money changes hands; device and receipt (write one: "Feb 14, iPhone 13 128GB, IMEI 1234..., $215, cash - signed by both") go in your records.

Laptops with stubborn resets can take the better part of an hour. If the clock is against you, the alternate choreography is: watch the seller *start* the reset in front of you, take the device, and finish verification at home with the clean-install steps in [Wipe It](09-wipe-it.md). Just know which choreography you are choosing before money moves.

## Source of the calm

The reason this is all scripted: you will not remember to check a headphone jack in a polite conversation. The worksheet remembers for you. Bring it, use it, and let your attention go elsewhere than memory.
