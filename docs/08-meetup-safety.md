# Meetup Safety for Buyers and Sellers

> Almost every used-tech transaction is just two people and one object in a parking lot. Keep it that way: public place, daylight, cash or a confirmed on-the-spot payment, and a phone that can call anyone. This chapter is short because the whole discipline is short.

## Where to meet (both of you)

Ranked, from best:

1. **A police station's designated exchange spot.** Many departments host marked "Internet Purchase Exchange Location" zones - lit, monitored, sometimes filmed. Nobody has ever regretted choosing one. Search your city or town's police site for one, or call the non-emergency line and ask.
2. **A busy, well-lit public place with tables and parking:** library steps, big-box store lots near the entrance, chain coffee areas in daylight.
3. **Indoor cafes** - good for phones and tablets (tables, Wi-Fi in the room, resets can run while you have a seat). Not for monitors: you cannot plug a 32" panel into a cafe without a comedy of extension cords.

Fine print, stated without fear:

- Because daylight works, daytime Saturdays are the safest and most common; the 9 p.m. parking lot is a decision worth talking yourself out of.
- Never either home address, at least not until something close to a relationship exists. For monitors too big to test elsewhere: meet in a big-box store lot during daylight, bring your own cable and laptop, and give it ten minutes of smoke-test on the curb before money moves. Big lots have foot traffic, light, and room; garages and back alleys do not.
- When the device is pricey, bring a second human even for twenty minutes. There is no discount worth meeting in private.

## How to pay (buyers)

- **Cash, after all tests.** The whole flow is: tests pass -> agreed number -> wipe ritual -> cash counted in *both* people's sight -> handshake. Nothing before the tests; nothing before the wipe.
- **On-the-spot app payments are fine** if *both* phones show the confirmation on screen *in person*: Zelle/Venmo/Cash App each have a "sent" state you can read together. Screenshots are not confirmations; you are both looking at the app together.
- **Never** wire money, pay by gift card, or - obviously - pay crypto to a stranger for a device. Chapter [Decoding Listings](01-decoding-listings.md) runs the whole scam pattern list.
- **The overpayment scam** runs against sellers (see below) - buyers just need to know it exists to recognize the seller politely falling for it mid-meetup.

## How to get paid (sellers)

- Accept cash; count it. Checking bills openly for texture and watermark takes half a minute and does not insult anyone.
- With app payments, watch the "Payment received" state *on your own phone* while standing with the buyer. **The overpayment scam** looks like this: buyer "accidentally" sends $400 for a $150 device (often with a fake screenshot or a real payment from a stolen account), then urgently asks you to refund the difference before their bank "reverses" the original. The refund is real; the original payment is not. Rule: *no refunds of any overpayment. Never.* The device leaves only when their real payment has landed whole, verified in your app.
- The wipe ritual runs **after** payment lands and **before** the handoff. Never wipe before payment; never hand off before the wipe. (That ordering is exactly in the seller's favor: you get paid for an unlocked, tested device, and no stranger walks off with your photo library.)
- If a buyer arrives pushy, agitated, or drunk: "I'll pass, but thanks for coming out." The listing will live again.

## The two-minute street checklist

Before you drive anywhere:

- [ ] Public place chosen - ideally a police exchange spot - and the seller has agreed to daylight hours
- [ ] A friend knows the who-what-where-when: name, platform handle, address, ETA home
- [ ] Location shared live (or a text every 20 minutes; agree the convention in advance)
- [ ] Phone charged, hotspot ready, exact cash on your person (buyers) / change ready (sellers)
- [ ] Worksheets printed or in phone notes; pen along
- [ ] The listing screenshot saved to your phone for price and photos

## Walk-away triggers (learn them tonight)

Leave immediately - no explanation, no debate, no guilt - when:

- Anyone asks you to come to a private residence, a "back of the building" lot, or a vehicle in an empty space.
- A second person appears who "is just a friend" and crowds behind you without introduction.
- They pressure you to pay *before* the tests, ever, under any story ("another buyer is on the way, just Venmo me the deposit"). That is not a hurry; that is the shape of a scam from [Decoding Listings](01-decoding-listings.md).
- Devices are shown with a story that keeps changing (any account number one moment, another the next).
- Feeling unsafe for a reason you cannot name. The feeling is a sensor that works; honor it. "I'm going to pass. Thanks for your time." Walk toward people and light, not toward your parked car alone.

**Nobody has ever been embarrassed by walking calmly away from a deal.** Embarrassment is only ever caused by the alternative.
