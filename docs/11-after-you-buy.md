# After You Buy: The First Days

> The deal is done, the device is wiped, and you are home before sunset. The next 48 hours decide whether this was a bargain or a story you tell in six months. This chapter is short on purpose - it is a landing, not a runway.

## The first evening (about an hour, all of it yours)

- [ ] **Wipe it again**, gently, for reasons explained in [Wipe It](09-wipe-it.md): accounts first, erase second, verify the clean setup screen.
- [ ] **Update the OS** before adding anything: security patches first, apps second, personality last. A 45-minute update now saves you weeks of odd behavior later.
- [ ] **Set it up fresh at your own pace** on your own Wi-Fi: your accounts, your passwords, your name in About. No seller's Wi-Fi networks or accounts ever remain.
- [ ] **Record your evidence**: the purchase date, price, meetup city, seller handle, and IMEI/serial. Email it to yourself; it is your resale story later, and your receipt story if it ever matters.
- [ ] **Case and screen protector**, same week, especially on glass-and-palm devices. $20 of protection against a $250 repurchase - the best cost-of-ownership math in this manual.
- [ ] **A good charger and cable** from a name you recognized in a store (certified USB-C/Lightning parts, based on the device's charging standard). One bad charging cable can taint the port, the battery verdicts, and your patience.

## The first two weeks: use it like you would use it

Breakage risks show themselves while the device is young in your hands; lean on it now for the same reason test drives run at full brightness ([Test Drives](06-test-drives.md)):

- Battery: watch the meter's honesty this fortnight (runtime per charge, charging speed at the wall); if it looks off from the meetup numbers, you have a *paper trail*, and a friend who sold you honesty may take it back politely.
- Heat, fan, ports: two or three busy evenings tells you more than any spec sheet.
- If anything was bought with a return window (open-box, refurbisher), *use it heavily now*, while the window is open. Find issues early - you are on a timer, and this is the week to lean.

## Battery-care habits for secondhand batteries

No myths, no snake oil - just the four things that actually matter for a battery that already has some years on it:

1. **Cold and heat are the enemy.** Park the phone out of the hot car; no laptop in the sun of a car's rear shelf. Batteries age in heat faster than they age in use.
2. **Charge habits are fine.** Modern devices manage the curve themselves; the 20-80% "optimization" features that many platforms offer do the work without you needing to fuss over it. Don't fuss.
3. **The calibration ritual is not real.** "Drain it to zero once a month" was advice for a battery era that ended years ago. Skip it.
4. **When the numbers drop:** battery health is a tool for *knowing*, not a reason to feel guilty. At 80% capacity years later, paying for a battery service is buying the machine another two full years - a smaller spend than any replacement device on this list.

## Resale-forward habits (for when you become the seller)

- Keep the box if the device ever came with it, plus receipts - "with box and receipt" is real resale money (see [Fair Prices](05-fair-prices.md)).
- Keep the purchase email your first evening created; buyers love a receipt story as much as you do.
- When *you* upgrade next year, this manual becomes seller-side: [Wipe It](09-wipe-it.md) is your chapter, from the other side of the table. Your wiped-with-a-receipt device is the listing buyers respect.
