# Negotiation Scripts for Calm People

> Haggling has a reputation for being combative. It is not. It is *swapping honest information to reach a number you both choose freely* - and almost every tense part of it can be avoided by having two or three sentences ready before you arrive. This chapter is those sentences.

## The mindset

Three beliefs make negotiation gentle:

1. **You adjust price only with facts.** "The battery reads 79%" and "comps sold at $190 and $210" are facts. "It's a bit beat up" is a vibe; leave vibes at home. Facts from [Battery Health](02-battery-health.md), [Screen Checks](03-screen-checks.md), and [Fair Prices](05-fair-prices.md).
2. **You are both allowed to say no.** The seller can decline; you can decline; nobody is harmed by a "no" politely delivered. If a deal only works when someone is pressured, it is not a deal.
3. **The relationship lasts ninety seconds more than the transaction.** Leave people feeling like they met a decent human. That pays you back in reputation, future deals, and sleep.

And the number one rule under all of it: **you negotiated nothing until every test has passed.** Price talk happens *after* the inspection, never before. Haggling before testing is negotiating against your own unknowns.

## Text-stage scripts (before you meet)

Keep these short. They filter sellers, set expectations, and can end negotiations before they start.

**Opening:**

> "Hi - is this still available? I'm a serious buyer: I can meet today, public place, cash."

**Price-setting early (use it when the listing is high):**

> "I looked at sold listings for this exact model and storage - they've been going for $190 to $230. Would you consider something inside that range? No worry either way."

**Expectation-setting (prevents meetup heartbreak):**

> "So you know how I work: I like to run a short checklist in person - battery percentage, a screen test, and the sign-out/reset. Nothing invasive. Sound okay?"

**Declining-by-text (when answers are worrying):**

> "Thanks for the details. I'm going to pass on this one, but good luck with the sale."

## The eleven scripts at the meetup

Each script below is one tool. Use the one the moment asks for; most meetups need two.

1. **The flaw-price script.** The workhorse. Name what you found, connect it to a number, stay open:
   > "It works and I like it. The battery reads 79% and there's a scuff on the corner. Would you take $190 for it? If not, totally fine - what's your best price?"

2. **The comps-anchored offer.** Facts first, number second, kindness last:
   > "Sold listings for this exact model - same storage, same year - have been closing around $180, $205, and $230. Given the battery's at 82%, I can do $200 today, cash, wherever you like."

3. **The split-the-difference.** After two rounds, when you are one polite breath apart:
   > "We're $30 apart. Want to split it and be done? $215, right now."

4. **The bundle ask.** When the price holds but extras float around (charger, box, case, pen, receipt):
   > "Keep the price - but throw in the original charger and box, and we're done."

5. **The silence.** After someone names a number, count silently to five. Most people fill silence with their better price. You do not have to say anything at all; it feels strange and it works - and it is the only script with *no words* in it.

6. **The walk-away line.** Your single most complete script; it leaves the door open:
   > "Today's just not the day for me at this number. If it's still around next week, maybe our timing works out. Nothing but good luck with the sale."

   Deliver it warmly, stand up, and actually leave. A walk-away that doesn't walk is an ultimatum.

7. **The pressure-neutralizer.** For "someone's coming at 6 with cash":
   > "I hear you. I only buy things I've tested, so I'll pass today - if it's still available Thursday, I'm a serious buyer."

8. **The ask-back (when you want the sale but at your number):**
   > "If it's still for sale on Friday, I'll pay $200 for it today, cash. If you'd rather hold out for a better offer, I completely understand."

9. **The yes-chain (when you have margin):** get two small yeses before the number:
   > "Can it be reset in front of me? [yes] Battery's at 86%, that's fine? [yes] Then: $205 today, cash, and I'm walking away happy."

10. **The seller-friendly opener (for private sellers who seem new):**
    > "This is a fair listing. I'm not going to grind you - let's just pick a number that works for both of us after I check it over."

11. **The hard line (kept for the rare moments it's needed):**
    > "I can't pay that, and I don't want to waste your morning. Thanks for showing me it."

Notice: **none of these lie**. They name tests, comps, and preferences plainly, then leave the yes or no to the other person.

## Reading the other side

A few patterns and the script each one earns:

| They say… | It means | Reach for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I've got someone coming in twenty minutes" | You are being hurried | The pressure-neutralizer, then leave happily |
| "It's worth way more than that" | They anchored by new price or memory | The comps-anchored offer; facts are less huffy than opinions |
| "My cousin says these go for…" | The real decision-maker is absent | "Come back when you can make the call" - politely, that's a fact too |
| "Cash offer, right now, quick" | They want to skip steps | The ask-back: tests first, always |
| "Final price" | Respect it or pass | The walk-away line, warmly |
| Silence after your offer | They are thinking (or about to fold) | The silence script; wait five beats |

## For sellers: the calm mirror

Selling? Every rule above works with the roles flipped - and it protects your price and your day:

- **Set your floor before the first message.** Write it down: "I will accept $X, no less." Deciding at the meetup is how people lose $60.
- **Disclose flaws in the listing** (chapter 1's honest-signals list): disclosed flaws sell *faster and calmer* than discovered ones.
- **Counter with the mirror of script 2:**
  > "That's a fair look. The battery is at 88% though, and the box and receipt are included, which keeps its resale value. I'm at $245 - meet me at $230 and it's yours today."
- **Say no warmly, once, and hold:** "I can't go below $240 - and honestly, if you watch comps for a week, you'll see why. No hard feelings either way."
- **Never argue with unreasonable offers.** "I appreciate the offer, but no - hope you find your phone" ends conversations where six paragraphs would not.
- **Payment in the right order:** cash in your palm (or the agreed app, confirmed on *your* phone), then the wipe ritual, then the handoff. [Meetup Safety](08-meetup-safety.md) covers the specifics.

## Practice

Once: read the flaw-price script out loud to your mirror, a plant, or a patient friend. It takes eight seconds, it feels silly, and it makes the real moment enormously easier - especially for people (the writer included) who find spontaneous negotiation draining. Scripts are what calm people use so they do not have to be sharp in the moment.
