# Decoding Listings: Red Flags and Honest Signals

> The listing is a story someone wrote to sell you something. Read it like a detective: your job is not to believe it or doubt it - your job is to notice which claims you can check at a meetup.

## The four buckets

Every listing detail you read falls into one of four buckets. Sort first, react second.

| Bucket | Meaning | Your reaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dealbreakers** | The device is unusable, stolen, or a scam | Walk away, no exceptions |
| **Yellow flags** | Questionable but possibly innocent | Ask, verify live, or factor into price |
| **Honest signals** | The seller is telling you useful things | Reward with realistic offers |
| **Scam patterns** | The device (or the seller) may not exist | Report and move on |

Dealbreakers, spelled out:

- "Still signed into my Apple/iCloud/Google account" or any wording meaning Find My / Factory Reset Protection is active. A device you cannot fully unlock survives nothing. See [Locks, Accounts, and Seed Phrases](04-locks-and-seed-phrases.md).
- "Locked to owner, forgot the password" - this *is* the dealbreaker above, phrased as an apology. It is a brick.
- "Blocked IMEI" / "bad ESN" / "not paid off" / "reported lost or stolen". WiFi-only forever.
- Swollen battery or "bulging a little at the seam". A safety hazard, not a discount.
- Payment first, device later - Zelle, Cash App, Venmo, gift cards, or crypto before you have held the thing.
- Any request to "ship it" after you agreed to meet locally.

Yellow flags that are worth a question rather than a walk-away:

- "Works great" with no model number or storage. Ask for both; the vaguer the listing, the more you need to the About screen at the meetup.
- "Battery is good" with no number. Numbers exist on every platform now (Guide 2). No number = verify in person.
- No charger, no box. Normal - but price it in (Guide 5).
- "Selling for a friend" / "was my son's". Common and usually innocent; just means: ask the questions anyway, and watch the unlock happen in front of the actual owner.
- "Screen is in a protector, never removed it." Fine until you wonder what it is hiding. Seen-in-person settles it.
- Recently relisted, price unchanged. Maybe nobody else saw the flaws you will. Come prepared to test.
- Screenshots instead of photos of the actual device. Ask for two real photos: one of the whole device, one of its About screen.

**Honest signals** - the listing style that usually means a smooth deal:

- Exact model, storage, color, and carrier status stated. (`iPhone 14, 128GB, unlocked, 89% battery, white, has box.` Every detail is a free fact you can check.)
- Their own photos, in the room the device lives in, of the *open* device - powered on, not just pretty product shots.
- Flaws volunteered plainly: "scratch top right corner, see photo 4". Humans who disclose scratches rarely hide worse things. That sentence is worth real money to you.
- A reason for selling: "upgraded", "company laptop off its lease", "medical device I no longer use". Ordinary reasons; stolen ones are vague.
- Shows the battery percentage or cycle count without being asked.

## Phrase-to-meaning decoder

Sellers are mostly honest people writing quickly. Here is the translation layer.

| They write | It usually means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Perfect condition" | One owner, careful, possibly no flaws | Verify with the screen test anyway |
| "Mint condition" | Almost new | Verify; ask what mint means to them |
| "Lightly used" | Lived in a bag | Check corners and battery |
| "Works fine" | Powers on; beyond that, unstated | Full test at meetup |
| "Just needs a screen" | Cracked glass, possibly digitizer damage | Price in the panel or walk; touch test after fix is impossible pre-buy |
| "Spares or repair" | Unknown problem, seller stopped diagnosing | Usually walk; only for hobbyists |
| "Was my brother's / for a friend" | You may be skipping the owner | Require unlock demo in person |
| "Overall it's fine, you know" | Vague confidence | Test everything; do not extend credit for warmth |
| "899 OBO" | Haggling room built in | Negotiation expected (Guide 7); "OBO" is permission, not a trick |
| "Cash only" | In-person, untraceable | Standard; pair with public meeting |
| "No tests, sold as seen" | You are expected to be rushed | Decline that deal shape; polite, then gone |

## The five questions (copy-paste them)

Message each seller these five before you plan any trip. Short, friendly, and they do not waste anybody's time:

1. "Hi - is this still available? What's the exact model, storage, and carrier status?"
2. "Why are you selling, if you don't mind my asking?"
3. "What's the battery health percentage - Settings > Battery > Battery Health?"
4. "Can you send a screenshot of the About/Settings screen, and a photo of the device powered on?"
5. "Is everything still signed in on it - iCloud/Google/whatever - and are you able to sign out before I buy? Nothing sinister, it protects us both."

How each answer grades you:

| Answer style | Read |
| --- | --- |
| Numbers, model names, "signed out already" | Honest seller, smooth deal ahead |
| Vague, deflecting, "works great!!" | Go anyway but test everything; price in some risk |
| Refuses screenshots, "just come look" | Optional; bring the worksheets and full skepticism |
| "Can't sign out, it's bought with a payment plan" | Dealbreaker - the phone is not fully theirs yet |

## Scam patterns (not devices, just cons)

These take zero hardware to run, so they run constantly. Spot the shape and you never need to see the device:

- **The too-good price.** A current flagship at 50% under every comp. Cheap is the bait; the price *is* the scam. See [Fair Prices](05-fair-prices.md).
- **The remote handoff.** "My nephew will bring it / I'm in another city Friday." You cannot run the locked/unlock test through a nephew.
- **The payment app dance.** Zelle first "to hold it", a QR code, a "deposit". Real meetups pay cash (or on-site app payments) after the tests.
- **The certificate.** Fake receipts, printed "warranty papers", screenshots of "Apple check" pages. Documents can be produced by anyone; devices unlock for their real owners.
- **The urgent story.** Deployment in 48 hours, listed twice in a week, "first come". Urgency exists to make you skip steps. Your steps are not expensive to skip-proof; just be stubborn.
- **The helpful seed phrase.** Any seller who mentions a crypto wallet, seed phrase, or "free wallet balance" on the device. Chapter [Locks, Accounts, and Seed Phrases](04-locks-and-seed-phrases.md) covers why you type nothing, ever, from stranger inputs.

## Honest-signal red flags (read twice)

Some listings fail tests you did not expect to run:

- The same model listed by the same seller many times ("multiple units", "wholesale lot"). Fine if they are a refurbisher, risky if they cannot answer basic battery-health questions per unit.
- Photos look like they were taken in five different houses. Ask which photo is *this* unit.
- Battery screenshots where the status bar is cropped out. Cropping is rarely innocent.
- "It charges, but only in one position." Loose charging port; known, priceable, but feels annoying to fix on a phone.
- "New" device, best-battery-health screenshot, but no box, no receipt, and can't say where they bought it. Trust the demo test more than the story.
