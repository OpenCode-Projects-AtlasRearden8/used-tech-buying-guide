# Fair Prices and Comps

> Used prices are opinion with a number attached. The market is full of listings priced by hope, guilt, and guesswork. Ten minutes of comparing and two simple formulas make you the most informed person in the conversation - which also makes you the calmest.

## Percent-of-new bands

The anchor most markets orbit: **what this device cost new, and how far the calendar has moved.** As used prices in general, these are US-market 2026-era ballparks for orientation - prices age fast, so use the bands as a starting argument, not a verdict.

| Age of the device | Typical range (condition-adjusted) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 1 year | 60-75% of new | Rare and fiercely held; almost no discounts unless flawed |
| 1-2 years | 50-65% | The best value and best risk trade for most buyers |
| 2-3 years | 35-55% | Sweet spot for quality machines nobody has mistreated |
| 3-4 years | 25-40% | Inspect with full skepticism; battery and screen drive the price |
| 4-5 years | 15-30% | For hobbyists and tinkerers; upgradeable models hold value |
| 5+ years | 10-25% | Parts machines, chromebooks, the "just needs love" tier |

Adjustments to bands that are nearly universal:

| Adjustment | Direction |
| --- | --- |
| Battery health below 80% on a phone/laptop | subtract roughly the local repair cost ($70-130 phone, $40-160 laptop) |
| Cracked screen (device otherwise perfect) | subtract 30-50% of the band |
| Non-original screen or battery part | subtract 10-30% |
| Missing charger | subtract $15-25 |
| Missing box | subtract $10 - minor unless the buyer resells |
| Carrier-locked phone (vs unlocked) | subtract $30-70 at mid-tier, more on flagships |
| Financing still active on a phone | not a discount - a dealbreaker (device is not fully theirs) |
| Original box + receipt present | add $15-40 in resale value later |
| Swollen battery | walk away; the price is wrong at any number |

For monitors, the decay is usually steeper: a 2-year-old 27" 1440p panel runs 40-55% of new; big 4K and ultra-wide screens fall faster (so many enter the market). Gaming monitors with refresh specs hold value better than office panels in the same size.

## Ten-minute comps

Nothing beats what the market *actually paid* (not what it *asks* - asking prices are fiction until someone pays them).

1. **eBay, sold listings.** Search the exact model and storage, filter to "Sold items" / "Completed listings", and read the last two or three weeks. Note 3-5 sale prices plus their condition notes and battery claims.
2. **Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist, current asks.** Same model, range from $0.5x to 2x your target price. You are reading sellers' hopes, but for *scarcity math* (how many exist nearby, how fast they seem to move).
3. **Swappa or the like (no affiliation, just a plain marketplace with a price guide).** Their sold/asking history is clean of scams and is a good second source for phones specifically.

Write your comp sheet like this, four lines, done:

```text
Model/Storage: iPhone 13, 128GB, unlocked
Sold comps:    $205 (89% batt) / $230 (91%, boxed) / $195 (83% batt)
Asking range:  $200-290 locally
My walk-in offer: $190 / walk-in target: $215 / cap: $235
```

That sheet is your entire negotiation until the meetup ends. Everything at the meetup adjusts from it: battery at 83% means your $190 has already got evidence behind it.

**The irresistible-price check.** A clean flagship at 50% under every comp is almost never a miracle. It is one of four things: Activation-Locked, blacklisted, a scam, or stolen. Guide 4 explains the first two; Guide 1 the others; and "stolen" answers itself through the meet-and-unlock discipline.

## Region and timing notes

- Prices in university towns spike every August-September and slide after spring graduation.
- New-model announcements drop last-gen prices in days; used prices drift down for weeks.
- Holiday diffs: January is the best buyer's month of the year (gift upgrades hit resale).
- Refurb-with-warranty sits a stable 10-20% above private-used pricing for phones. See [New or Used](10-new-or-used.md) for when that is worth it.

## Price sanity under pressure

A calm buyer needs one sentence to keep in the pocket:

> "I am not in a hurry, and I know roughly what these have sold for. If we can both pick a number inside that range, we have a deal. If not, truly no hard feelings."

That sentence is honest, factual, and repels exactly the conversations you do not want. Guide 7, [Negotiation Scripts](07-negotiation-scripts.md), shows how it unfolds in eleven scripts.
