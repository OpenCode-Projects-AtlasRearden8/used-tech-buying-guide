# Screen Checks: Ten Minutes That Save the Deal

> Screens are the part you will pay the most attention to for the next four years, and the part listings describe least accurately. A screen test takes ten minutes, needs no internet, and is almost impossible to fake in person. Carry a screen-checking routine everywhere you shop secondhand.

## Bring your own test colors

You do not need an app or the web. You need solid colors on a screen. Two ways to get them offline:

- **The offline color test in this repo.** Open `color-test.html` (from `assets/` here, or the copy built into the finished site) on *your* laptop or phone while shopping for a monitor. Put it on a USB stick for parking-lot meets: arrow keys step through white, grays, black, red, green, and blue.
- **Use the seller's own machine.** Open a blank note or document fullscreen for white; switch to a dark-mode app fullscreen for black; the gray steps and solid colors come from the color test tool when you have your own device handy.

On laptops specifically, a quick white test: open a full white browser page (about:blank works) fullscreen; a black test: any dark-mode app, fullscreen.

## The four screen sins

No two are the same problem; each has a different fix and a different price.

| Fault | How it looks | How fixable | What it means for the deal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dead pixel** | A permanently black dot, unlit, on every background | Unfixable unless the panel is replaced | One or two in a corner on an older panel = negotiable; any in bright central field = walk or heavy discount |
| **Stuck pixel** | A dot stuck on one color (or white/red/green/blue dot) over black | Sometimes fixes with pixel-cycling, usually not | Same treatment as dead pixels, but verify on more than one background color |
| **Burn-in** | A ghost of keyboards, status bars, or app layouts faintly visible on OLED/AMOLED panels | Not fixable, and it gets worse | Any visible burn-in on a phone = walk; negotiate hard on a tablet |
| **Backlight bleed / clouding** | Glowing patches in corners or blotchy clouds on black pages | Mostly a manufacturing quirk, not fixable | Mild is normal on laptops; heavy patches = discount, on monitors especially |

Panels also get:

- **Dead digitizer zones**: touch does not register in one strip (usually an edge or the area near a previous crack).
- **Tint problems**: yellow or pink cast at low brightness, or a color gradient across the panel.
- **Brightness decay**: the panel dims prematurely or flickers at low brightness (PWM dimming on many phones flickers slightly at low brightness - some flicker is normal, constant visible flicker is not).

## The ten-minute screen test in order

Run them in this order; it is organized so the slowest tests happen while you talk, not while you wait.

1. **Power on, full brightness.** Drag the brightness slider all the way up. A panel that still looks dim at 100% has a wrong setting or a dying backlight.
2. **White test.** Fullscreen white on a clean surface. Look for: dead dots, blotchy clouds, corners brighter than the middle, and any bright spots in the center (these highlight a tired backlight quickly).
3. **Black test.** Fullscreen black (the color test tool's Black, or a dark-mode app fullscreen). In a normal room, look for: cloudiness, corner glow, and on OLED, ghost images of whatever was left on screen most ("burn-in").
4. **Gray suite.** Three grays (light/mid/dark), with the color test tool or a page with gray blocks. Gray is where uniformity problems, tint shifts, and burn-in humiliations appear. Spend a minute on each, tilting your head slightly; the patterns that appear and disappear with viewing angle tell you an LCD's quality better than any spec sheet.
5. **Scratch hunt.** With the screen off (black), hold a light source at an angle (a phone flashlight at arm's length works): micro-scratches appear as thin bright lines. This is how you find the scratches that screen protectors were "always on" for.
6. **Touch sweep** (phones/tablets/laptops with touchscreens). Open a drawing app or the notes app and drag a finger slowly across *every* edge, all four corners, and the middle on a diagonal. Every part you touch must leave a trail. Missed strokes = dead zones.
7. **Flicker/glitch.** Let the screen sit on the gray for a couple of minutes while conversation happens. Panels with failing electronics show thin horizontal lines or momentary color pops as they warm.

## OLED-specific (most phones, some tablets, monitors)

- Burn-in is never going away: keyboard layout ghosts, notification bar ghosts, and "the same icon, but faintly" are permanent wear.
- Test with a mid-gray and a dark-gray screen and *look from a little distance for 30 seconds* - the ghosts only appear on uniform, dim backgrounds.
- **Green line issue:** some AMOLED panels age into a vertical green/pink line at the edge. If you see a faint line along the edge, treat it as a screen-arm problem; it is not a settings quirk.
- Badge burn is normal-ish on phones (clock, status bar, keyboard) - shallow, mild badge ghosting on a $150 phone is a discussable discount, not a dealbreaker. Deep ghosting = walk.

## Laptop screens specifically

- **Hinges: stop here if they lift.** Open the lid: the hinge should hold the screen open at any angle without assist, fall gently, and *not* lift the bottom plate. A lifting hinge is the most common silent laptop killer on old models.
- **Uniformity:** dark fullscreen + white fullscreen tells you the "dim corners" story; mild is normal, blotchy is discount.
- **Color cast problems:** a laptop screen stuck yellow/pink regardless of settings may also be a broken gyroscope/auto-brightness cache. If Color settings don't fix it, price the panel as tired.
- **Anti-glare coatings on old ThinkPads/MacBooks can wear off** as shiny spots - cosmetic, negotiate lightly.

## Phone and tablet screens specifically

- **Touch digitizer over cracks** - if a screen has replacement glass history (or a hairline crack), touch should still test 100% even after a repair; discount heavily on history you cannot test.
- **True Tone is a claims test:** on an iPhone, True Tone that no longer kicks in after a third-party screen = non-original panel. Demand original or discount accordingly.
- **Screen protectors:** politely ask "Can we check the screen underneath the protector? Absolutely no rush." Most sellers peel it if they have nothing to hide - and peeling is *their* option, not yours.

## Monitor tests specifically

Monitors get the longest screen exams in the world; use them.

1. **Bring your color test** (USB stick or a laptop running `color-test.html`): white, gray scales, black. Uniformity problems, dead pixels, and bleed read fast at monitor resolution/size.
2. **Brightness spread:** run brightness from 100% down to the lowest step. Severe visible flicker at low brightness (some PWM dimming is invisible; the kind you can *see* is fatiguing for years) decides how much you will enjoy the monitor long-term.
3. **Warm it up.** Monitors home themselves in the first 10 minutes: capacitor or driver-board issues show up after warmup, when the OSD reports some activity. Let it sit on white while you chat idle for 10 minutes - lines, tint shifts, or pops as it warms are red flags of a tired board.
4. **Ports**: bring your own cable (HDMI or the brand's DisplayPort) + a laptop that drives the panel. Turn the OSD buttons one by one; loose OSD buttons eventually fail.
5. **Stand and VESA**: wobbles at each range; VESA holes present, no broken mounts. A naked stand wobble is fine, a *broken* stand does not sell itself cheap.

## How much a screen is worth

| Problem | Deal? | Price it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light micro-scratches, uniform panel | Buyable | -5 to -10% |
| Deep scratch over visual field | Negotiate | -10 to -20% |
| 1-2 dead pixels, edge, old mission panel | Buyable | -5% |
| Dead pixels center-field | Walk or heavy discount | -30% |
| Visible OLED burn-in on phone | Walk | - |
| Screen replaced with non-original part | Discount or walk | -10 to -30% |
| Cracked glass, touch still perfect | Only if you fix screens | -30 to -50% |

A screen replacement price (screen glass on a phone $70-250 installed, a laptop panel $60-150 plus labor) is your reconciliation number. If you will pay for that panel anyway, subtract that estimate and the "free" becomes obvious.
